# Assistance and evidence policy

Revision: `assistance-policy-v1`

This policy separates access support from answer-revealing help. The distinction is important for SEND practice, fair progress decisions, and honest adult reporting.

## Access support

Audio replay, audio/reading support, reduced motion, high contrast, simple text, visual guides, switch access, keyboard response, large targets, focus mode, and adult-operated controls are recorded as context. They do not invalidate independent evidence and do not reduce mastery simply because a learner used an access route. They are support needs or response routes, not evidence of a deficit.

Runtime adaptations are also recorded through the mission and lesson-step events. The attempt ledger accepts only the server allow-list, so unknown client labels cannot alter grading or mastery.

## Answer-revealing help

Hints, answer models, worked examples, answer-revealing audio, and phoneme audio explicitly marked by content as answer-revealing are persisted separately. Correct answers using those forms of help are not counted as independent correct evidence and receive a modestly reduced mastery gain. The answer is still recorded honestly and remains useful for the next teaching decision.

Required or intrinsic listening and ordinary instructional audio are not answer-revealing by default. Explicit replay/support actions can be recorded as audio context without changing correctness.

## Reporting and safeguards

Adult-facing evidence views show whether an attempt was independent or supported and list the recorded assistance. Pupil views do not expose private grading evidence. Assistance telemetry is UI context, not proof that a learner attended, understood, or used a control continuously; retained and varied evidence remains necessary for mastery and progression decisions.

This is an implementation policy and QA gate, not a substitute for human safeguarding, SEND, curriculum, or listening review. Human approval remains required before production promotion.

The aggregate JavaScript gate is now `1,406,000` bytes for this batch, a narrowly documented increase to accommodate the adult evidence presentation. The per-route JavaScript, largest-file, CSS, and public-asset limits were not raised.
